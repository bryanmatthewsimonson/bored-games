# Testing guide

How to play Chain Reaction, Chess and Bank locally, with real people, and how to run the tests. Commands run from the repo root.

## 1. Local play: 3 to 6 players in one browser

Chain Reaction takes 3 to 6 players. The steps below use three; for more, open one more window per player (`profile=d`, `e`, `f`) and choose that many players at **Create table**.

Home lists **Your games**, then the **Game catalog** (a search box plus filters for player count, genre, mode, length and complexity), then the **Open tables** of every game. Each game has its own page (`#/games/<id>`): what the game is, its facts, **How to play**, the **New table** form, and its open tables and your games of it. Under the facts, Chess and Bank link to their own **BoardGameGeek** pages; Chain Reaction, Luster, Right of Way, Driftwrights, Holler and Room for Doubt each say **Compare to** the published game they implement, linked to that game's BoardGameGeek page, with a note that the platform is not affiliated with its makers (D053, D060, D066, D072, D078, D080). Their cards say "Compare to" too, and typing a published title in **Search games** finds its game. Bank is a folk game: its page links to BoardGameGeek and does not say "Compare to".

You need Node 22.18 or later and pnpm 10 (`corepack enable` provides pnpm).

1. Run `pnpm install`.
2. Run `pnpm dev`. This starts the in-memory dev relay on `ws://localhost:7777` and the app on `http://localhost:5173`. Ctrl-C stops both. The relay keeps nothing, so restarting it loses every table and game.
   - **Ports are fixed.** Vite runs with `strictPort`, so if port 5173 (or the relay's 7777) is already taken, `pnpm dev` stops with an error instead of moving to another port. Stop the other process (often an earlier `pnpm dev`) and run it again.
3. Open **three separate browser windows side by side, not tabs**, one per player (one more per extra player):
   - `http://localhost:5173/?profile=a&relays=ws://localhost:7777`
   - `http://localhost:5173/?profile=b&relays=ws://localhost:7777`
   - `http://localhost:5173/?profile=c&relays=ws://localhost:7777`
   - for a bigger table, the same with `profile=d`, `e` and `f`

   Each profile has its own key and storage, so these are separate players. The header shows `profile: a`, and so on.
   - **Why windows:** browsers throttle timers in hidden tabs, which slows the automatic shuffle and deal to a crawl. If a window seems stuck, click into it to focus it.
   - **Why `&relays=`:** it saves `ws://localhost:7777` as the profile's only relay, so local tests stay off public relays. Without it, `pnpm dev` uses the dev relay **and** the default public relay (`wss://relay.primal.net`). The setting is saved per profile, so later visits need no `&relays=`; **Settings → Reset to defaults** restores the default list. Only `ws://localhost[:port]` and `ws://127.0.0.1[:port]` are accepted here, and only by `pnpm dev` (a dev server) or a build made with `VITE_ALLOW_LINK_RELAYS=1` (as `pnpm e2e` does). Any other relay in the link, or any `?relays=` on the published site, is ignored, and the page says "Ignored relays from the link; change relays in Settings."
4. **Create** (window a): in the **Game catalog** on Home, click **Chain Reaction** to open its page. Under **New table**, choose **3 players** (the default; up to 6 are possible) and a **Time allowed per move**, then click **Create table** (for 3 players the form shows "2 open seats" before you create it). The Table page opens and says "Waiting for 2 more players."
5. **Join** (every window but a): the table appears on Home under **Open tables**; click **Join**. The page then says "You are seated."
   - To join with the link instead, click **Copy share link** in window a. The link carries no profile, so add one before pasting it into window b: `http://localhost:5173/?profile=b#/t/…`. Then click **Join this table**.
6. **Start** (window a): once the page says "Every seat is taken.", click **Start game**, then **Yes, start the game**. All the windows move to the game.
7. **Play.** The status bar at the top says whose move it is ("Your move: place a tile.", or "Waiting for npub1… to …"). In the window whose move it is:
   - **Place a tile:** click a tile under **Your tiles**, or pick it in **Place a tile**, then click **Place …**. Hovering over a tile previews where it lands.
   - **Found a chain**, **Choose the surviving chain** and **Order the defunct chains** appear when a placement calls for them.
   - **Buy shares and end your turn:** enter up to 3 shares in total, then click **End turn**.
   - **Merger disposal ("Your … shares"):** sell, trade 2 for 1 or keep, then click **Confirm**.
   - **End the game:** once an end condition holds, **Buy shares and end your turn** shows a **Declare the end of the game** checkbox. The results then show "Audit: checking the hidden moves…" and then "Audit passed".
   - The **Log** panel lists what happened, newest last (the last 100 lines).
   - **Price card** (above the Chains panel) opens the share prices and the majority and minority bonuses for every size, with each chain on the board marked in the row of its current size. **Rules** opens the player rules in a new tab.
   - **Rules** in the header (and **How to play** on a game's page) opens the rules of the game at hand at `#/rules/<game>` (`#/rules/chain-reaction`, with the price card; old `#/rules` links still open it).

### Chess (2 players)
Chess has no hidden cards, so there is no shuffle or deal: the board appears as soon as the game starts.
1. Run `pnpm dev` and open two windows, `?profile=a&relays=ws://localhost:7777` and `?profile=b&relays=ws://localhost:7777`.
2. **Create** (window a): find **Chess** in the Home catalog (type "chess" in **Search games**, or pick **2** players) and click it. Its **New table** form offers 2 players only; click **Create table**. **How to play** on the page opens the Chess rules (`#/rules/chess`).
3. **Join** (window b) and **Start** (window a) as above. Window a plays White.
4. **Play:** click one of your pieces, then the square to move it to (a dot marks a legal move, a ring a capture), or drag the piece there; from the keyboard, Tab to the board, move with the arrow keys and press Enter or Space to pick up and put down (Escape drops the piece). The board is flipped for Black. A pawn reaching the last rank opens a **Promote the pawn to** dialog (Escape or **Cancel** keeps the pawn). Tick **Offer a draw with this move** to offer a draw with your next move; your opponent then sees **Accept draw**. The move list (in algebraic notation) and each side's captured pieces are beside the board, or below it on a phone.
5. **Resign** (under the board) asks for confirmation first, then ends the game: you lose. Before the first move, resigning cancels the game instead.
6. At the end both windows show the result and "Result confirmed: signed by both players." once both apps have signed it.

Fool's mate is a quick test: 1. f3 e5 2. g4 Qh4#. The piece art is the Cburnett set (BSD-3-Clause); its license is on the **Credits** page, linked from the footer.

### Bank (2 to 6 players)
Bank has no hidden cards and no shuffle. The dice are fair because every seat publishes a share of the roll, and every app derives the same faces from those shares (D058). A short game is three windows and five rounds.
1. Run `pnpm dev` and open three windows, `?profile=a&relays=ws://localhost:7777` and the same with `profile=b` and `profile=c`. For two players, stop at b. For more, add `d`, `e` and `f` and pick that many players.
2. **Create** (window a): type "bank" in **Search games** and open **Bank**. Its page links to BoardGameGeek and has no "Compare to" line. Under **New table**, choose the player count (2 to 6; 3 is a good trial), **Rounds** (5, 10 or 20; 10 is the default) and **Banking**. **Everyone may bank** asks each player still in the round, and the player about to roll answers last. **Only the roller may bank** asks that player alone. Click **Create table**. **How to play** opens `#/rules/bank`.
3. **Join** and **Start** as for Chain Reaction. The felt shows the round ("Round 1 of 5"), the pot and the seats. There is no shuffle line.
4. **Play.** One button is enabled at a time. A round opens on the roller, and the only button is **Roll**: the pot is empty, so nobody is asked to bank or stay. After a roll, **Bank** takes the current pot and sits you out for the rest of the round; the pot stays for the others. **Stay** leaves you in. The felt counts the rolls this round and marks how many of the three safe rolls are left. Beside the buttons, a table lists what the next roll can do to the pot, and that table changes after the third roll. The first three rolls of a round cannot bust: a 7 adds 70, and anything else, doubles included, is added. From the fourth roll a 7 wipes the pot, and doubles double it.
5. **The dice.** After **Roll**, the other open windows send their part of that roll on their own. There is no extra button: everyone is deciding on the same dice. The faces then appear on all three windows. A window that stays closed can still hold its part back, and the others can claim that player's timeout for it.
6. A round ends when a 7 busts it after the third roll, when everyone has banked, or after 30 rolls (everyone still in then banks the pot). The next round's dice move to the next seat. The highest total wins; a tie shares first place. At the end every window shows the same winner line and "Result confirmed: signed by all N players."
7. **Resign** is the same control as in Chess. With 2 players you lose, and the result counts. With 3 or more the game ends for everyone, unrated, and you come last. Resigning before any roll cancels the game.

The dice are drawn as SVG. A window that asks to reduce motion shows them without the short tumble.

### What to expect
- **Setup takes some seconds, and longer with more players.** After the start, each client shuffles the deck and proves the shuffle, then deals; the players shuffle one after another, so every extra player adds a step, and every client checks every step. The screen shows "Shuffling the deck: 1 of 3 players done.", "Dealing the tiles…" and "Working… this can take a few seconds." A deck with a second shuffle round (Room for Doubt, D076) goes round the table twice, and the line says which round: "Shuffling the case files (round 1 of 2): 1 of 3 players done." Measured by `pnpm e2e` with all the windows on one 4-core machine, from **Yes, start the game** to the first move: about 11 s with 3 players, 16 s with 4, 28 s with 5 and 43 s with 6. Expect about 15–30 s with three players on a laptop, and about a minute with six. Every player's window must be open on the game for its share of the work to happen.
- **Turns are asynchronous.** Nothing hurries a player. Close a window whenever you like: reopening the game (from Home, **Your games** → **Open game**) rebuilds it from the relay and the secrets saved in that profile.
- **Merger decisions can come to you out of turn.** When a chain is taken over, each player holding its shares disposes of them in turn order. That form can appear in a window whose turn it is not, so check every window when the game seems to wait.
- **A new tile shows as "?" for a while.** The tile is yours as soon as you draw it, but it stays hidden until every other player has made their next move: each move carries that player's decryption share for it, so no one, not even the app, can see your tile without you. It is always revealed before your next turn while the game goes on, and in async play that can take hours. Hover over or tap the "?" (or focus it and press Enter; Escape closes it) for a popover with this explanation; a one-line note under the hand says the same while a tile is hidden. If a timeout ends the game first, a tile never revealed stays a plain "?" with no popover. (A faster reveal was tried and withdrawn, since one cheating player could expose another's tile; D039, D042.)
- **Players are shown by name, avatar and short npub.** A player who set a name in **Settings → Name and picture** (their NOSTR kind 0 profile) shows as "Ann (npub1…)" in the status line, the log and the timeout messages; one without a name shows by short npub alone. The Players panel and the final results put each player's avatar beside the name: their picture, or a generated pattern from their key when they have none. A name changed mid-game shows within seconds in every open window.
- **Other players' shares and cash are hidden.** As at a real table, the Players panel shows another player's holdings only as chain chips without counts, and their cash only as "has cash" or "no cash". Your own row is exact, and the Chains panel's sizes, prices and bank supply are exact. The log shows other players' counts and amounts only for the current and the previous turn; older lines say "Ann bought Jade and Lapis shares." Everything is shown at game over (D037).
- **Who the game waits for (D057).** While the deck is shuffled and dealt, the setup screen names whoever the game is waiting on, by name and short npub: "Waiting for Ann (npub1…) to shuffle. Their app must be open on this game.", and during the deal every player still missing their deal shares ("Waiting for Ann (npub1…) and Bo (npub1…) to send their deal shares."). In play the status line already names the player to move; when a move waits on another player's shares, or the end of the game on a player's end-of-game secret, a line above the board names them the same way. A player whose name shows there must open the game in the app and browser they joined with.
- **"You're watching this game" (D057).** A window whose key holds none of the seats is a spectator, and now says so on the game screen, in setup and in play. To try it, open the game link in a fresh profile (`?profile=w`): with no record of the table and no tables of its own, it says "You're watching this game. This browser's key (npub1…) isn't one of its players. If you joined from another app or browser on this device, open the game there: each one keeps its own key." A profile that plays other games and has no record of this one gets only "You're watching this game.". If one of the profile's **Other keys** (Settings → Identity) holds a seat, it says "You joined this game with npub1… (kept in this browser)" with a **Switch to npub1… and reload** button. If the profile holds game keys for the table made for a key it no longer has, or joined the table but was not seated, it says that instead.
- **"Stuck: an automatic step failed"** means a shuffle, deal or secret step failed. Reloading that window retries it.
- **Home badges.** On Home, **Your games** shows a **Your turn** badge for a game that was waiting on you when this profile last had it open. It is not a live inbox: a game this profile has not opened for a while shows "Open to check".
- **Resigning.** Every game offers **Resign** under the board, with a confirm step. With 2 players (Chess) you simply lose. With 3 or more it ends the game for everyone (D052). To try it, play a few turns of a 3-player Chain Reaction game as above, then press **Resign** in one window (it need not be that player's turn): the confirm step says "Resigning ends the game for everyone…"; click **Yes, resign**. The other two windows send their end-of-game secrets automatically (the line reads "Ended early: … resigned · unrated. Checking the game…" for a moment), then every window shows "Ended early: <name> resigned · unrated. Final places: …" with the resigner last, a **Final results** table with each player's cash as if the game ended now, and "Result confirmed: signed by all 3 players.". Such a result will not count toward ratings. Before the first move, resigning cancels the game instead. `pnpm e2e` does this at the end of `play.spec.ts`.
- **Timeouts.** Once a player's move deadline (1, 3 or 7 days, chosen at **Create table**) has passed, the other players get a **Claim timeout** button in the status bar (or on the setup screen). It asks for confirmation first (**Yes, claim the timeout**) and explains the result: the stalled player forfeits and the game ends at once, or, before the first move after the deal, the game is cancelled. Nothing is claimed automatically. The deadlines are too long to try this in a short local session.

### Game names: trademark-safe or licensed (D046)
Every build shows the trademark-safe names (Chain Reaction, Jade, Lapis…). The original names of the game Chain Reaction implements are a licensed brand pack, kept out of the published site until they are licensed: **Settings → Game names** there says "Only the trademark-safe names are available on this site."

To try the licensed pack locally:
1. Stop any running `pnpm dev`, then run `VITE_LICENSED_BRANDS=1 pnpm dev` and open a window as in step 3.
2. **Settings → Game names** now offers **Trademark-safe names** and **Original names (licensed)**, each with examples. Pick one: the choice is saved per profile (`bg:<profile>:branding`) and applies at once, in an open game too.
3. Check that the switch reaches every place a name appears: the catalog card and the game page (title, tagline, summary; the catalog search also finds the game by the licensed aliases, such as "hotels"), the board's cell names (screen reader), the Chains, Players and holdings panels, the decisions, the status line, the log, the **Price card** and the rules page (`#/rules/chain-reaction`). The chains' colors and label letters change too (D053): the original names bring the original hotel colors and each hotel's initial, while the fill patterns stay the same under both, for color-blind play. Switch back and forth in an open game (or on `#/dev/board/mid`) and check that the board cells, the chain swatches in the panels and decisions, and the **Price card** all follow.

Only what one player sees changes: the games, their events and the other players are unaffected. `#/dev/board/mid` in a dev server shows a game screen without playing one. Never deploy a build made with the flag: the public build scan (`pnpm scan:build`, part of `pnpm check`) proves that the normal build holds none of the licensed names, and the Pages workflow builds with the flag off and runs `pnpm scan:dist` before uploading.

## 2. Playing with real people

### Deploy to GitHub Pages
1. In the GitHub repository, open **Settings → Pages**. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **GitHub Actions**. You only do this once. A private repository needs a plan that includes Pages.
2. Merge to `main`. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs `pnpm check`; once it passes, the **Deploy web app to GitHub Pages** workflow (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) builds `apps/web` and deploys it. Nothing deploys while CI fails. You can also start the deploy by hand from the **Actions** tab.
   - **Default branch.** A `workflow_run` workflow runs from the repository's default branch, and the `github-pages` environment allows deployments from it. Keep `main` the default branch (**Settings → General → Default branch**).
3. Share the URL the workflow prints, usually `https://<owner>.github.io/<repo>/`. Each person plays in their own browser, so no `?profile=` is needed.

**Before sharing widely, use a dedicated origin.** A project site at `https://<owner>.github.io/<repo>/` shares its origin, and so its `localStorage`, with every other GitHub Pages project site of the same owner. Each player's identity key and game secrets live in that storage, so any script on any of those sites can read them. For anything beyond a test among friends, serve the app from a custom domain or subdomain, or from a Pages user or organization site (`<name>.github.io`) used only for this app (D036).

### Relays
- The deployed app uses one default relay for everybody, `wss://relay.primal.net` (D038). Players can add more in Settings.
- **Large events.** Each player's shuffle step is one event of about 36 KB, and many public relays cap event size or rate-limit. `wss://relay.primal.net` accepted and served back signed 8, 36 and 60 KB events in a probe on 2026-10-01. If you add other relays and a game stays on "Shuffling the deck" while every window is open, a relay is refusing it (PLAN open question 7).
- **Changing relays:** click **Settings** (top right). Under **Relays**, type a `wss://…` URL, click **Add**, then **Save relays**. Use **Remove** to drop a relay and **Reset to defaults** to restore the list. The list is saved per profile. A link cannot set your relays on the published site: `?relays=` works only with `pnpm dev` or a build made with `VITE_ALLOW_LINK_RELAYS=1`, and even then accepts only local `ws://localhost` and `ws://127.0.0.1` relays.
- A table records the creator's relays when it is created, and its game events go to those relays. A joiner must use at least one of them to find the table. Otherwise the Table page says "This table has not turned up on your relays yet." So agree on relays before creating the table.

### Optional: log in with a browser extension (NIP-07)
Install a NIP-07 extension (for example Alby or nos2x) and reload the app. In **Settings → Identity**, tick **Use browser extension (NIP-07)**. The page reloads and you play as the extension's key. That is a different player from the profile's local key. The extension asks you to sign the table, the join, the game start and the end-of-game attestation. In-game moves are signed with a per-game session key, so they need no prompt.

If you chose the extension but it is not there when the page loads (disabled, or injected too late), the app uses the profile's local key and says "Browser extension not found; using this profile's local key." Without an extension, the app creates a local key per profile. **Settings → Identity → Show secret key (nsec)** shows it so you can export it.

### Names, pictures and keys
- **Settings → Name and picture** sets the name, about line and picture that other players see beside your npub (your NOSTR kind 0 profile). A picture can be an uploaded photo (cropped to 256×256 and re-encoded without its metadata), one of the gallery pictures, or an https link. Uploads go to the Blossom server `https://blossom.primal.net`; change it under **Picture server**. Until you add a name, Home suggests it.
- **Settings → Identity → Use a secret key from elsewhere** imports an `nsec`. Every key you replace is kept in this browser under **Other keys**, with **Switch to**. Games stay with the key that joined them: Home marks another key's tables "Under another key", and they cannot be joined from the current key (D041). The import is refused in a private window, where nothing would be saved.
- On the first table you create or join, the browser is asked to keep this site's data, and Settings shows whether it agreed. Home reminds you to back up your key until you copy it or tick "I've saved it".
- **Backup before joining or creating a table (D057).** Joining a table (**Join this table**, **Accept invitation**, or **Join** in a list of open tables) or creating one with a local key that was never backed up first asks "Copy your secret key first?", with **Copy secret key** (copies the `nsec` and counts the key as backed up, as Settings' **Copy nsec** does; the button then reads **Join** or **Create table**), **Join anyway** (**Create anyway**), **Cancel**, and a **Don't ask again for this key** box. Once the key is backed up, or the box was ticked, it is not asked again. A browser extension (NIP-07) keeps its own key, so its users are never asked.
- **A key that is not being saved (D057).** In a private window, or with site data blocked, joining or creating a table first says "This browser isn't saving your key, so you could lose your seat. Allow site data (not a private tab), or back up your key first." There is no way on until **Copy secret key** succeeds, or the key is shown (**Show the secret key instead**) and **I've saved it somewhere safe** is ticked; then **I've saved it: join** (or **…: create the table**). The key is taken from the page's memory, so this works even when the browser refuses to store it. The page banner says "Your key is not being saved. This browser is blocking site storage, so a reload or a closed tab loses your key and your seats." If the stored key changed since the page loaded, a join or create is refused with "Your key changed in this browser; reload." (or, when no key is stored at all, "Your key is no longer saved in this browser. Reload, then back it up."), with **Copy this page's secret key**.
- **A lost key (D057).** If a profile that had a key (tables, kept keys, a backup, or the record of its last key) loads with no key, the app makes a new one and shows a red-bordered banner, "Your previous key was not found in this browser, so a new one was made. Games you joined before may need it.", until **Dismiss**. To try it: in a profile with a table, delete `bg:<profile>:sk` in the browser's developer tools (Application → Local Storage) and reload.
- **A key changed in another tab (D057).** Open two tabs of the same profile; in one, import a key (Settings → Use a secret key from elsewhere). The other tab immediately covers the page with "Your key changed in another tab of this site. Reload to continue." and a **Reload** button, and signs nothing more (a hidden tab reloads by itself). The old key is kept under Settings → **Other keys** first, so **Switch to** brings it back. This guards against the race behind the incident: two pages starting at once on a fresh browser each made a key, and one played with a key the browser did not keep. Browsers with Web Locks (Safari 15.4+, current Chrome and Firefox) cannot make two keys at all; the others rely on this guard.
- **A seat played with saved game keys (D057).** A browser that still holds a seat's game keys (`bg:<profile>:secrets:<table>`) plays that seat even when its player key is another one now: the game screen says "Playing seat N with the game keys saved in this browser (you joined as npub1…; this browser's key is now npub1…)." Moves, shuffles, deals, secrets, timeouts and Resign work as usual; only the final result is not signed by that browser (that needs the joining key), and the result stands without it. If the joining key is in **Other keys**, the notice offers **Switch to npub1… and reload**. Home shows such a table with its usual badge and "Playable with saved game keys". To try it: join a table in profile `c`, then in developer tools replace `bg:c:sk` with another valid 64-hex key and reload.
- **Your key on a second device (D065).** Joining (or creating) a table backs up that seat's game keys (session key and deck secret) to the table's relays and yours, as a NIP-78 event encrypted with NIP-44 to your own key. Open the game in another browser that holds the same nsec (Settings → Use a secret key from elsewhere): it shows "Restoring your game keys from your backup…", then "Your game keys were restored from your backup, so you can play your seat here. Play on one device at a time: …", and plays the seat. Both devices can play the seat, but not at the same moment: each asks the relays before it signs, yet two devices acting within seconds can still sign two rival moves (D063's residuals), and a turn saved on one but never sent is dropped once the other has played it (D056). With no backup on the relays, the screen says so, tells you to open the game on the device you joined with (which backs it up when it opens the game), and offers **Try again**. To try it: `?profile=a` creates a table, `?profile=b` joins and it starts; in another browser (or another browser profile, not a private window, where an import is refused) open the game link with `?profile=a`, import a's nsec, and reload. `pnpm e2e keys.spec.ts` does this with Chess.
- **The backup is checked (D065, review M1).** Each time the device that joined opens a game, it looks for its backup on the game's relays and publishes it again if it is missing or wrong; below the game it says "This game's keys are backed up on its relays." with **Back up again**. With a browser extension, Settings and the backup button warn that any site allowed to decrypt with the extension (NIP-44) could read the backups, play your seats and see your hidden cards.
- **Other keys lists every key whose games are here (D065).** Settings → Identity → **Other keys** lists the keys this profile used before (with **Switch to**) and also every key whose game keys this browser holds but which is not kept here ("not kept in this browser"), each with its games in progress and **Open game**: such a seat is played with the saved game keys (D057). Home never says "switch to it in Settings" for a key that is not kept: it says the game can be opened and played with this browser's saved game keys instead.
- **Settings on a phone (D057).** The Settings dialog fits the screen: its title and **Close** stay at the top while the rest scrolls inside the dialog (scrolling up inside it never pulls the page to refresh), and a second **Close** sits at the bottom.
- **In-app browsers (D057).** Opened from a link inside Facebook, Messenger, Instagram, LinkedIn, Snapchat, TikTok, Line, WeChat, X (Twitter) or the Google app, or in any Android WebView (which includes Gmail's on Android), the page shows "You're in an in-app browser. Your player key lives in this browser's storage and may be lost. Open this page in Safari or Chrome to keep it." **Dismiss** hides it for this tab only. Apps that open links in a view reporting the system browser's own user agent (SFSafariViewController on iOS, which several apps use, including some versions of the X app; Gmail on iOS, whose in-app view has no verified marker; Chrome Custom Tabs on Android, which share Chrome's storage anyway) cannot be told apart and get no banner, and neither does a home-screen copy of the site on iOS. To see the banner on a desktop, set a user agent in the browser's developer tools, for example one containing `Instagram 290.0.0.13.76`.

## 3. Known limitations
- **Game keys are backed up, the player key is not.** A seat's game keys are backed up to your relays, encrypted to your key (D065), but your player key itself lives only in this browser: copy it from Settings. A game joined before the backup existed is backed up once its joining browser opens it again (a NIP-07 extension: tap **Back up this game's keys**). An extension without NIP-44 support cannot back up or restore.
- **No "your turn" notifications.** Home's badge only reflects games this profile has had open (section 1).
- NIP-46 remote signers are not supported.

## 4. Running the tests
- **`pnpm check`** runs typecheck, Biome lint and every Vitest project (engine, deck, protocol, client, relay, dev relay, web, brand, fuzz smoke and repo guards). The repo guards include the public build scan (`tests/public-build.test.ts`, alone with `pnpm scan:build`): it builds the web app as GitHub Pages does, into a temporary directory, and checks that no file, source maps included, holds a restricted name or licensed pack string in any case, then checks that a `VITE_LICENSED_BRANDS=1` control build holds them all. The exceptions are the exact phrases "Compare to <reference title>" for Chain Reaction, Luster, Right of Way and Room for Doubt (D053, D060, D066, D078, `ALLOWED_PHRASES` in `tests/restricted-names.ts`): the scan lets them through, and checks that the public build holds each of them whose game the app hosts; the title alone, in another case or inside another word still fails. `pnpm scan:dist [dir]` runs the same scan over an existing build (default `apps/web/dist`) without building; the Pages workflow runs it before every upload. Run it before every commit. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs it on pushes to `main` and on pull requests.
- **`pnpm e2e`** runs every end-to-end browser spec in `apps/web/e2e/`: `play.spec.ts`, `profile.spec.ts`, `chess.spec.ts` and `bank.spec.ts`. The main one, `play.spec.ts`, is described here. It starts a dev relay and `vite preview` on free ports. The players (3 by default, one browser context each) then create, join, start and play at least two full rounds through the UI, on until a merger disposal. One player reloads mid-game. All of them must agree on the board and the turn at the end. Then the third player resigns, and all of them must show the same unrated result ("Ended early: … resigned · unrated"), the places with the resigner last, the results table and every signature (D052). It is not part of `pnpm check`.
  - The first time on a new machine, run `pnpm --filter @bored-games/web exec playwright install chromium`. The dev container already has the browser.
  - `E2E_SEATS=6 pnpm e2e` plays with 4, 5 or 6 players instead of 3 (values outside 3–6 are clamped). It plays 2 full rounds and one more turn, and the time allowed for the setup and for the whole test grows with the seats. With 6 players it takes about 2 minutes, with the setup at about 43 s (see "What to expect").
  - `E2E_FINISH=1 pnpm e2e` plays to the final results and a passed audit (about 3 minutes).
  - `E2E_SCREENSHOTS=/some/dir pnpm e2e` saves a screenshot per player. `pnpm e2e --headed` shows the browser.
  - `apps/web/e2e/profile.spec.ts` (a few seconds) checks names and pictures with the Blossom server intercepted: one player uploads a photo carrying EXIF data, the other picks a gallery picture, and each sees the other's name, picture and npub on the table seats. `pnpm e2e profile.spec.ts` runs it alone.
  - `apps/web/e2e/chess.spec.ts` (a few seconds): two players find Chess in the Home catalog by searching, open its page, create, join and start a table, play Fool's mate (both see the checkmate and both attestations), then a second game ends by resign. `pnpm e2e chess.spec.ts` runs it alone; with `E2E_SCREENSHOTS` it saves the picker and the board mid-game, at mate and after the resign at 1280 and 390 px.
  - `apps/web/e2e/bank.spec.ts`: three players find Bank by searching, open a 3-seat table of 5 rounds with **Everyone may bank**, roll, and see the dice with no further tap, then bank until the game ends. All three show the same dice and the same winner line, and the rules page says the roll is the same for every player. The felt does not scroll sideways at 390 px. `pnpm e2e bank.spec.ts` runs it alone.
- **`pnpm sim --game <id>`** (any game with a fuzz target, for example `chess`, `chain-reaction`, `bank` or `room-for-doubt`) plays whole games between independent clients over an in-memory relay (`--adversary vanish|resign|equivocate|…`; `--adversary resign --vanish-at <n>` resigns at the seat's first decision once the chain holds n moves, with the secrets and the partial audit in Chain Reaction); `pnpm test:sim` runs the long Chain Reaction sim tests. `pnpm sim --game bank --games 20` plays Bank, dice beacon included, and `pnpm sim --game room-for-doubt` plays Room for Doubt at 3 to 6 seats, sealed Verdict shares included. The move deadline is `--deadline <seconds>` when given, else the `simDeadline` of the game's fuzz target (`tools/fuzz/src/index.ts`), else one day. Room for Doubt names 3 days: at one day, a long 6-seat game can fork on the documented timeout-claim race, because the sim's random scheduler may leave a seat that owes a dice share unpicked for a simulated day (D078).
- **`pnpm build:web`** builds the static app into `apps/web/dist`, as the Pages workflow does.
- **`pnpm fuzz --games 1000`** plays random games against the rules engine and checks its invariants (`--games 10000` for the full run). The default game is Chain Reaction. `pnpm fuzz --game bank --games 10000` fuzzes Bank (seat counts 2–6). `pnpm fuzz --one "<seed#i>" --players N` replays one failing game; add `--game bank` for a Bank seed.
